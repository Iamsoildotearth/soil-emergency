UK Soil Emergency — Urgent Need for Stronger Action Beyond Current Targets

Dear Secretary of State / Minister / [Specific Name, e.g. Chair of the EFRA Committee],

I am writing as a concerned [constituent / citizen] to urge you to treat soil degradation as the national emergency it truly is. Healthy soils are the foundation of food security, biodiversity, water regulation, climate resilience, human nutrition, and long-term agricultural productivity. Their continued decline poses profound risks to the UK’s economy, environment, public health, and national security.

The Government has taken some positive steps. The Environmental Improvement Plan 2025 commits to bringing at least 40% of England’s agricultural soil into sustainable management by 2028, rising to 60% by 2030. It also includes publication of an initial soil health statistic in 2026, a fuller baseline by 2029, and principles for sustainable soil management. The Sustainable Farming Incentive (SFI) 2026 offers voluntary payments for actions such as cover crops, herbal leys, and no-till farming. The JNCC Indicators of Soil Health (2026) provide an early modelled assessment, with arable soils currently scoring around 61.9% in supporting sustainable productivity.

However, these measures are widely regarded as insufficient to reverse ongoing soil degradation or prevent a full soil health crisis. The targets are modest, non-statutory, and heavily reliant on voluntary uptake. Key gaps remain: there is still no dedicated statutory Soil Health Strategy with binding targets, no comprehensive independent national soil survey, and limited mandatory standards. Some practical soil assessment actions have even been removed from SFI 2026.

Without far stronger and faster intervention, the consequences will be severe:

  • Declining nutritional quality of food: Soils low in organic matter and microbial life produce crops with significantly reduced levels of essential vitamins, minerals, and phytonutrients. Analysis of the UK government’s own official data — McCance and Widdowson’s The Composition of Foods — reveals dramatic declines between 1940 and 2002:

CarrotsMagnesium75%You would need to eat roughly four modern carrots to obtain the magnesium in one carrot from 1940.

Food today simply does not deliver the same nutritional value as in previous generations. This is contributing to poorer public health outcomes and risks long-term decline in the nation’s health and resilience.

  • Increased food insecurity and higher food prices due to declining yields and greater reliance on volatile imports.
  • Heightened flood risk and water quality problems from poorer water infiltration and increased runoff.
  • Accelerated biodiversity loss and reduced carbon storage, undermining climate goals.
  • Long-term damage to agricultural productivity and rural economies, with potential multi-billion-pound costs to the UK.

I therefore respectfully urge you to go significantly further and ask:

  1. When will a statutory Soil Health Strategy with legally binding, science-based national targets be introduced?
  2. When will soil health be formally recognised as a strategic pillar of UK food security and public health policy?
  3. When will a comprehensive independent national soil survey be commissioned to establish a robust baseline?
  4. How will the Government strengthen ambition beyond the current 40%/60% targets to achieve widespread soil restoration by 2030?
  5. When will a cross-departmental Soil Emergency Working Group be established to coordinate urgent action?
  6. What steps will be taken to move from voluntary incentives to a stronger mix of regulation, investment, and accountability?
  7. When will a robust framework for independent verification and public reporting on soil health progress be fully implemented?

I would be grateful for a detailed response on these points and any additional actions you plan to take or support. Soil health is too critical to be addressed with incremental measures alone.
